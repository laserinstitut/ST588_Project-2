The Melbourne housing dataset contains 34,857 records and 21 variables describing property characteristics, locations, sale methods, and prices. Among records with observed prices, the median price is A$870,000 and the mean is approximately A$1.05 million. The higher mean and maximum price of A$11.2 million suggest a right-skewed distribution. Prices are missing for 7,610 records (21.8%), so price analyses will use only records with observed prices. Building area and year built also have substantial missingness, and some values warrant further investigation, including construction years ranging from 1196 to 2106 and unusually large land and building areas. The analysis will explore how prices vary across property types and regions and how they relate to distance from the city center, building area, and room count.

Questions to explore:

1. How do prices differ across property types and regions?

2. How does the mix of property types vary across regions?

3. How is price associated with distance from the city center?

4. How are building area and room count associated with price?

priced <- housing |>

The histogram shows that observed property prices are concentrated around A$1 million, with fewer properties at the low and high ends. On the logarithmic scale, the distribution appears roughly mound-shaped. I used a log scale because prices range widely, and a small number of expensive properties extend the upper end of the distribution. The log scale compresses those high values and makes the spread of the more common prices easier to see. The axis labels still show prices in Australian dollars, but equal distances on the axis represent equal multiplicative changes rather than equal dollar amounts.
